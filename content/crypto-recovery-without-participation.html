Crypto’s Narrow Rebound: Why Relative Strength Is Not a Recovery

A market with only six advancing assets out of a hundred, persistent ETF redemptions and rising derivatives turnover deserves a harder test than a bounce in Bitcoin.

November 24, 2026 · Investigative analysis

Bitcoin climbed back above 60,000 USDT late on November 24. That was the encouraging part of the afternoon. The less encouraging part was that, in a separate market-wide snapshot, only six of a hundred selected crypto assets had posted positive returns over the preceding 24 hours.

What stands out to me is the distance between a visible price rebound and the breadth of demand behind it. Bitcoin can recover a round number while most of the market is still deteriorating. A handful of tokens can outperform Bitcoin without attracting enough new capital to change the market's direction. Neither observation is a contradiction; both are reasons to be careful about calling a recovery.

My interpretation is bearish on the underlying structure, with moderate conviction, but considerably less certain about what caused the selling or what happens next. The evidence is more persuasive about the condition of the market than about its next move.

Institutional capital is still leaving the ETF channel

The more consequential demand signal is not a single price print but the persistence of redemptions. Across twenty reported sessions from October 27 through November 23, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows of $5.019 billion and Ethereum ETFs $1.498 billion. The last five sessions contributed $2.368 billion and $382.3 million respectively in net redemptions.

The macro backdrop is not uniformly hostile. The November 20 two- and ten-year Treasury yields were 4.63% and 4.92%, both lower than on November 16. But the October 28 Federal Reserve target remained 3.75–4.00%, October CPI rose 0.4% month over month, and unemployment was reported at 4.1%. Lower yields could reflect easing expectations or defensive demand; they do not by themselves establish easier financial conditions for crypto. The November 23 equity rebound is likewise not proof of renewed digital-asset demand.

More derivatives turnover, less decentralized spot turnover

DefiLlama's November 24 dashboard reported $50.134 billion of seven-day DEX volume, down 5.69% on the preceding week, alongside $197.819 billion of perpetual turnover, up 5.24%. That is a divergence worth investigating, not a global spot-versus-futures ratio: the two tracked universes differ and omit centralized spot activity.

More perpetual turnover could mean more hedging, new leverage, liquidations or simply more churn. At roughly 17:57–17:58 UTC, indicated eight-hour OKX funding was positive for BTC (+0.005883%) and ETH (+0.005061%), but negative for SOL (−0.003352%). Open interest was approximately $1.839 billion, $1.151 billion and $205 million respectively on those three OKX swaps.

These are not global totals. More importantly, static open interest cannot reveal whether positions are expanding or closing. The observed spot-perpetual midpoint discounts of roughly 5.6–7.6 basis points shortly after 18:00 UTC establish a local pricing relationship, not a mark-index premium or evidence of forced liquidation. I cannot distinguish spot-led selling, short positioning, covering and forced deleveraging without aligned native open-interest changes, aggressor flow and liquidation observations.

Source: DefiLlama · OKX market data

Liquidity is a separate question from turnover

A narrow spread can hide a shallow book. On OKX, six historical spot snapshots of twenty price levels on each side showed a particularly important difference between advertised turnover and the notional visible close to the midpoint. At 18:00 UTC, HYPE's returned book contained only about 7,687 USDT of bids and 3,473 of asks. VVV showed about 57,413 of bids but 19,370 of asks. Neither snapshot describes the full exchange book, much less the entire market.

Network activity is not the same as token demand

Stablecoin capitalization was around $281.1 billion, down 1.11% over seven days, while aggregate DeFi TVL stood at $68.755 billion, down 1.12% over 24 hours in the November 24 dashboard. A later same-day reading differed slightly; the exact observation clocks are unknown. Stablecoin contraction may reflect redemptions or migration, and falling USD TVL can arise from declining collateral prices rather than withdrawals.

Solana presents a useful counterexample to a blanket contraction narrative. Its seven-day DEX turnover rose 7.07% even as tracked stablecoin capitalization fell 3.16%. Ethereum mainnet DEX turnover declined 17.31%. That could be a shift in usage, faster turnover of existing liquidity or speculative trading. It is not evidence of net capital migration by itself. Ethereum layer-two activity and staking economics also matter; raw transaction counts are not comparable across chains without reconciling their definitions.

Protocol revenue is equally easy to misread. Hyperliquid's reported trailing 30-day protocol revenue was $77.89 million, compared with $8.24 million for Uniswap and $6.40 million for Aave. These figures are economically interesting, but protocol receipts, token purchases, burns and legally enforceable holder claims are different concepts. I would not convert them into a valuation multiple until the underlying rights and circulating-supply economics are reconciled.

Aave's TVL rose 8.17% over seven days despite broader weakness. That is an important counterpoint to systemic-deleveraging claims. At the same time, BTCS disclosed on November 23 that it had sold approximately 27,300 ETH and repaid $43.8 million of Aave borrowing after the November 20 decline. This is a real example of balance-sheet deleveraging, not proof of an ecosystem-wide liquidation cascade. Precautionary debt reduction remains a plausible explanation.

Four research subjects, not four recommendations

The selective strength that persists through a decline deserves scrutiny rather than automatic promotion. Venice Token, TRON, Hyperliquid and XRP illustrate distinct mechanisms. None is cleared by relative returns alone.

Venice Token (VVV) had gained 23.55% over seven days in the 17:41 UTC snapshot, while Bitcoin fell 5.91%. Its inference-credit model provides a possible demand mechanism, but contract permissions, actual credit use, emissions and verified burns determine whether that mechanism is economically meaningful. The shallow historical ask book is a separate concern. Venice tokenomics documentation.

TRON (TRX) was comparatively resilient across seven and thirty days. Its resource staking and transaction burn mechanism is documented, but network usage does not automatically translate into net token demand, especially where issuance and governance concentration remain important. TRON economic model.

Hyperliquid (HYPE) warrants attention because of its documented fee-funded purchase and burn mechanism, despite negative seven-day relative performance and declining technical structure. Vesting and circulating supply could materially change the economics. Hyperliquid fee documentation.

XRP showed seven-day relative resilience but thirty-day underperformance. The ledger's original 100-billion maximum supply and escrow mechanism are documented; current distributions and actual settlement demand still matter more than a fixed maximum in isolation. XRP Ledger escrow documentation.

These are unranked research subjects. Their supply, security and liquidity evidence does not support an execution-quality assessment.

The distinction that matters

I would revise the bearish structural assessment if sustained recovery above nearby resistance were accompanied by a positive median return and a broadening majority of advancing assets, followed by improvement in daily trends and institutional flows. A convincing explanation of the underlying selling would require a different set of evidence: aligned spot and derivatives flow, native open-interest changes and documented liquidation activity. Neither price movement nor turnover supplies that evidence on its own.

The important distinction is between a market that is capable of bouncing and a market that has repaired the conditions that made it vulnerable. November 24 offered evidence of the former. The latter remains an open question.

Evidence and methodology

Market breadth is calculated on the same 100 selected constituents using reported percentage changes, excluding stablecoins and recognized economic duplicates. Sector definitions are provisional; current constituents introduce selection and survivorship limitations. Moving averages use completed native OKX candles. ETF totals cover reported US sessions through November 23 and may be revised. Dashboard figures are dated but not synchronized. The historical candle summaries, order-book snapshots and aggregate quotes are single-source observations rather than independently authenticated records. No live execution or causal attribution is inferred.
