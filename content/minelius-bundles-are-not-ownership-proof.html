MINELIUS and the Wallet-Cluster Claim That Outran Its Evidence

A Solana launch drew a startling bundled-buy warning. Conflicting scanner verdicts show why purchase patterns, holder balances and beneficial ownership must not be mistaken for one another.

Research cutoff: November 22, 2026 Eastern / early November 23 UTC · Planned article date: 2026-11-22 · Snapshot evidence, not a live token rating

The headline number attached to a newly launched Solana token was hard to ignore: 79.31% of its tokens had reportedly been purchased through bundled buys. The same token's trading pool displayed a liquidity-lock reading of 100% in one scanner and 0% in another. Neither number, on its own, identifies who owns the coins or who can remove the liquidity. That distinction is the MINELIUS story. GeckoTerminal [S01]; DexAlerts [S02].

MINELIUS is a Solana token identified by mint E9rWKjVhHMxQkxfREr7Li8Yy46rVcWiWHoKVQAe9pump. Its principal observed MINELIUS/SOL pool was 7yJDP2VmgazVEa6usbXM61uSk5AT3J4dZFaJQvq2LnfZ on PumpSwap. The identifiers match across the two scanners; this article does not treat other same-name coins as interchangeable. Pool record [S01]; token record [S02].

What a bundle warning actually tells us

GeckoTerminal's security text reports that 79.31% of the tokens were purchased via bundled buys. That is a vendor classification of purchases, not an independently disclosed list of beneficial owners and not a current percentage of supply controlled by a connected group. The page does not provide, in the material examined here, a complete transaction-indexed list, cluster membership, funding ancestry or sale proceeds from which to reproduce its percentage. The observation is therefore REPORTED [S01/C03], rather than an independently recomputed on-chain fact. GeckoTerminal security explanation.

The warning is worth taking seriously. Coordinated purchases at launch can distribute supply across accounts in a way that looks, on a holder leaderboard, less concentrated than the underlying economic exposure. Yet a bundle does not automatically mean that the same person controls every buyer. Transactions arriving near each other may be bundled by shared infrastructure, bots, or other mechanisms. Establishing common economic control requires stronger links: shared non-service funders, deliberate transfer chains, joint exit destinations, documented operators or other corroboration. No such wallet-level reconstruction was supplied in the available evidence.

A separate Larpcheck report alleged that 34 funded wallets held 63% of the token. That more specific allegation remains UNKNOWN as an on-chain conclusion: the accessible report did not expose all wallet addresses, transaction hashes or balances necessary to reproduce the calculation. It is not plotted as a current ownership fact, and neither number is added to the other. [S05/C06]

Trading is visible; common ownership is not

At an early-November-23-UTC page capture, GeckoTerminal displayed 32,639 buys and 17,572 sells over a rolling 24-hour window. I recomputed their sum as 50,211 transactions, the same total the page displayed. That is evidence that sells are occurring through the pool; it is not evidence that every wallet can exit in unlimited size, that trades are independent, or that net dollar flow is positive. Transaction counts are not dollar volumes. GeckoTerminal snapshot [S01/C01/C02/CALC01].

Liquidity's two meanings

The apparent contradiction sharpens at the LP check. GeckoTerminal describes liquidity as 100% locked; DexAlerts labels 0% locked/burned. These are conflicting reports about the same named market, not two measurements that should be averaged. GeckoTerminal [S01/C04]; DexAlerts [S02/C05].

The protocol's own documentation explains a likely interpretation problem. In the canonical Pump.fun migration flow, bonding-curve liquidity moves to PumpSwap and the resulting LP tokens are burned, while the PumpSwap accounting distinguishes recorded LP supply from tokens directly burned outside a withdrawal. That is a program-level mechanism—not a transaction-level inspection of the exact MINELIUS pool's LP mint, holders or migration signature. So the strongest defensible statement is narrower: the canonical migration design burns LP; this pool's complete position-level lock state was not independently reconstructed. Pump program documentation [S03]; PumpSwap pool documentation [S04].

A market cap is not an exit pool

GeckoTerminal displayed approximately $8.19 million market capitalization and $212,422.49 pooled liquidity in the same retrieved page. Dividing the latter by the former yields 2.59%—a ratio of displayed metrics, not a prediction of slippage or a claim about the amount of capital available to sellers. The market-cap input is rounded by the source, making the derived percentage approximate. GeckoTerminal snapshot [S01/R04/R05/CALC02].

The countercase matters

There are observations that cut against a simple "cannot sell" allegation. The GeckoTerminal page lists thousands of sell transactions. DexAlerts reports the mint and freeze authorities revoked and a top-10 holder share of 24.7%. That share is a different denominator and a different scanner snapshot from GeckoTerminal's bundled-buy percentage; comparing them as proof of inconsistency would be a mistake. No native binary decode of the token's Token-2022 extensions was obtained here, so successful ordinary sells do not clear every possible transfer privilege. DexAlerts [S02/C07]; GeckoTerminal [S01].

Likewise, a historical scanner report of zero direct developer holdings would not negate the possibility of holdings in linked accounts, while a developer warning exceeding ten percent cannot be treated as proof without the address, time, and observed token-account balances. GeckoTerminal itself posts a more-than-10% developer warning, but the available evidence does not resolve which wallet population or methodology produced it. The warning is a prompt for investigation, not an established concentration figure. [S01/C08]

A platform claim adds a separate trust question. The Minelius site has described an engine holding mine-wallet keys and a private-launch arrangement that obscures the user's wallet from launch history. Those descriptions, if applicable to this specific token's operation, would make attribution harder; they are not evidence that any identified launch buyer is controlled by the company. The claims are documented in the earlier research memo but were not tied to an independently verified on-chain payout trace. Minelius project site [S06/C09, site-reported].

What would falsify this assessment?

A transaction-indexed launch ledger could establish the set of first-block purchases and distinguish a true common-funder cluster from merely contemporaneous automated trades. From there, independently decoded token balances, SOL funding hops, swaps, and withdrawal destinations could support—or disprove—the reported 34-wallet cluster. A replay of the exact Pool account, LP mint and migration transaction could settle the lock-status dispute without trusting either percentage label. A full mint-account decode could check every enabled Token-2022 extension rather than infer absence from the ordinary trading record.

For each potential linkage, the test must preserve transaction signature, block/slot, instruction index, wallet address, amount and units, and whether the link is a direct transfer or a heuristic. A payment to the same exchange deposit infrastructure does not, by itself, identify a common person. Addresses can be investigated without guessing or exposing private people behind them.

Methods and evidence status

OBSERVED in retrieved pages: the text and figures published by GeckoTerminal and DexAlerts on the exact mint/pool pages [S01–S02]. REPORTED: all vendor-labeled ownership, security and bundle classifications [C03–C09]. CALCULATED: trade-count reconciliation and the ratio of two co-displayed USD metrics [CALC01–CALC02]. INFERRED: common-owner attribution cannot be deduced from these reports. UNKNOWN: raw bundle-member wallets, shared funders, realized exit proceeds, current cluster ownership, exact LP token holder balances and complete mint extension data.

Snapshots were retrieved during November 22, 2026 Eastern / early November 23 UTC, without a reproducible vendor timestamp for every metric. Figures preserve the page capture's stated 24-hour window rather than imply immutable live values. No figures show wallet-level flows, and no unlabeled same-name tokens are included. The original PROMPT ZERO v3 A–G dossier and READY figure handoff were unavailable; these two narrowly scoped figures were newly validated against the directly retrieved vendor rows, not presented as preapproved on-chain forensic graphics.

MINELIUS demonstrates how a security dashboard can be simultaneously useful and insufficient. One scanner raises a credible question about bundled purchases; another offers a narrower holder statistic; two disagree about liquidity accounting. The responsible conclusion is not that the wallet farm has been proved, nor that the token has been cleared. The next meaningful evidence would be the transactions and balances beneath the percentages.
