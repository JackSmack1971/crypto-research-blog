Aptos Unlock Calendars Still List Investors After Vesting Ends

One November calendar projects 14.38 million APT, including investor and insider allocations. Another projects 4.54 million. The original vesting arithmetic explains much of the gap—but the Foundation’s separate 210 million APT staking commitment poses a harder question.

ARTOfficial IntelligenceNovember 8, 2026Research cutoff: November 8, 2026

Aptos says its original investor and contributor vesting cycle is finished. Yet a prominent public unlock calendar continues to assign November tokens to investors and insiders. The discrepancy is not a rounding error. It changes the apparent size and composition of the next supply event—and invites a larger question about how confidently the market should treat the numbers displayed on token-unlock dashboards.

The opposing estimates are striking. Tokenomics.com lists 14,377,312 APT for November 12, 2026, with 19.5% attributed to private investors and 27.5% to insiders. DefiLlama instead lists approximately 4.54 million APT for November 11, with community and foundation allocations. Blockworks independently displays 4.54 million APT for November 12 and marks the original investor and contributor categories fully unlocked.

Dates, release definitions and data-provider assumptions can differ. But the first test is simpler: do those purported investor tranches still exist under Aptos’s published genesis schedule?

What the evidence says

  • Aptos publicly states that genesis investor and core-contributor vesting concluded October 12, 2026.
  • The original schedule yields approximately 4.543 million APT per month for continuing community and foundation releases.
  • The extra investor and insider portions in Tokenomics.com’s November projection closely resemble the old monthly vesting installments.
  • A separate Foundation commitment to permanently stake 210 million APT is documented, but a publicly reproducible account-level proof of permanent restrictions was not established in the available evidence.

The original vesting terms are the useful starting point

Aptos published its tokenomics overview at mainnet launch in October 2022. Of the one billion APT minted at genesis, 510.217 million were assigned to the community, 165 million to the Foundation, 190 million to core contributors and approximately 134.783 million to investors.

The first two groups followed extended ten-year distribution schedules. The latter two had a twelve-month lock, six accelerated monthly installments, and then smaller monthly installments until their allocations were exhausted. The contract described a four-year lockup horizon; the arithmetic establishes why the final installment need not occur on the fourth anniversary itself.

For each investor or contributor allocation, the published installments sum to the full grant:

6 × (3/48) + 30 × (1/48) = 1

That is six accelerated installments and thirty ordinary installments. Under the publicly described schedule beginning in October 2023, the last ordinary installment falls in September 2026. October 12, 2026, then marks the formal fourth anniversary. The distinction helps explain why some coverage calls September 12 the last investor and contributor release while Aptos calls October 12 the end of the cycle.

On October 13, the official Aptos account stated that the schedule had concluded the previous day and that no genesis investor or core-contributor tokens remained to unlock. This is an attributable issuer assertion consistent with the original formula—not an independently reconstructed list of final beneficiary transactions.

Where the 9.83 million APT disagreement comes from

Once the investor and core-contributor schedules are complete, the unchanged original community and Foundation installments total about 4,543,478 APT monthly. That matches DefiLlama and Blockworks within ordinary rounding. Tokenomics.com’s 14,377,312 APT is about 3.16 times as large, a difference of roughly 9,833,834 APT.

The larger calendar’s recipient percentages offer a telling numerical fingerprint. Its reported 19.5% investor share implies roughly 2.80 million APT; its 27.5% insider share implies roughly 3.95 million. Those figures closely match the old investor installment of 2.808 million and core-contributor installment of 3.958 million.

That correspondence supports a plausible interpretation: the model may be extending an expired schedule. It does not establish why the provider calculates the larger number. Its community component is also about 3.07 million APT above the original community installment. Tokenomics.com includes staking rewards in a broad community classification, raising the possibility that the forecast incorporates estimated emissions or another accounting category. A provider-level formula, dated source inputs and classification rules would be needed to prove the exact cause.

There is a second denominator problem. Tokenomics.com’s page describes 2.1 billion APT as the overall scheduled supply through 2044. But the 2.1 billion figure is a hard supply cap, whereas the original one-billion genesis distribution and subsequent staking emissions are different mechanisms. Counting long-horizon potential issuance as locked scheduled vesting risks presenting a ceiling as if it were an already minted treasury inventory.

Unlock, issuance, distribution and sale are not synonyms

This is where unlock coverage often loses precision. A vesting event changes an entitlement or transfer restriction. Staking rewards create new token supply. A Foundation grant redistributes an existing balance. An exchange deposit transfers custody. An actual sale creates market flow. None of these events is interchangeable, and the same token can appear in multiple stages without representing multiple new units of supply.

Aptos also changed its emissions economics in 2026. AIP-140 proposed reducing staking rewards and imposing a 2.1 billion APT cap. Later issuer updates describe a 2.6% reward rate and cap as implemented. These changes matter for forward supply modeling but do not themselves revive expired genesis investor grants.

The most defensible reading of the 4.54 million figure is therefore deliberately narrow: it reconstructs the monthly community-and-Foundation schedule under the original terms, assuming those schedules remain applicable. It is not proof that 4.54 million newly sellable tokens will reach exchanges in November.

The separate claim: 210 million APT permanently staked

Alongside the unlock milestone, the Aptos Foundation announced that it would permanently stake 210 million APT and support its operations using the resulting staking rewards instead of selling the principal. In principle, that is a consequential change in the supply story. Permanently restricted principal should not be treated as ordinary future sale inventory.

But the word permanently requires more than a pledge. Standard staking arrangements can include owner-authorized unlock and withdrawal functions, subject to protocol rules. Demonstrating that tokens are presently staked is not equivalent to demonstrating that the owner—or another authorized controller—cannot ever withdraw them.

Blockworks’s October 20 research report described the 210 million APT as locked and counted it against undistributed Foundation and community holdings. That is an important published research claim, but the material available for this investigation did not establish a Foundation-authenticated mapping of the relevant staking accounts, deposit transactions, principal balances, and irrevocable withdrawal restrictions.

A particularly revealing technical check concerns governance proposal #183. It has been cited in connection with the Foundation’s tokenomics reform. The published Move source for its supply-cap voting step identifies an empty governance proposal used for voting. Its execution does not, by itself, prove that 210 million tokens were transferred into a non-withdrawable arrangement.

That is not evidence that the Foundation failed to carry out its commitment. A separate lock could exist. It does establish a limit on the common shortcut of citing the executed governance vote as sufficient proof of the permanent custody restriction.

What would establish the lock?Authenticated Foundation-controlled account addresses; a complete balance reconciliation totaling 210 million APT; transaction hashes and ledger versions for the relevant deposits; deployed Move modules or resource-account authority records; and an explanation of whether any admin, owner, or upgrade path can release the principal. Staking reward balances should be identified separately.

Why the remaining 210 million question changes the analysis

Blockworks estimated approximately 335 million APT remained unvested in October, with 210 million supposedly permanently staked, leaving about 125 million for future distribution. Its implied remaining annual release pace was lower than the straightforward 54.52 million APT annualized from the original community-and-Foundation installments.

Those figures should not be collapsed into one definitive schedule without reconciling which locked holdings overlap the original vesting buckets. If a permanent lock takes effect on balances already counted as future unlocks, a calendar can overstate sellable supply even if its mechanical vesting schedule is accurate. If, by contrast, the commitment covers previously circulating or separately classified stake, subtracting it from the remaining vesting schedule may double count the restriction.

The distinction is economically meaningful. A permanently restricted token and a token freely withdrawable after a staking cooldown have different potential market behaviors. Yet neither behaves exactly like a burn while it remains in supply and continues earning staking rewards. The principal may be restricted while the yield is spendable—precisely the arrangement described by the Foundation’s operating model.

What the calendars can—and cannot—say about price

At a November 8 snapshot price of approximately $0.5952 per APT, the smaller estimate represents roughly $2.70 million of notional tokens; the larger one about $8.56 million. Those are illustrations based on the snapshot, not forward prices or forecasts of market impact. A calendar estimate is not a sell order.

Even an accurately scheduled release does not reveal when holders will transfer, lend, collateralize or sell. A serious liquidity assessment would require the actual recipient accounts, transfer events, exchange inflows, bid and ask depth across venues, and evidence of subsequent execution. None of those can responsibly be inferred from a calendar countdown.

The market may still price expectations before an event. That makes bad classification consequential: a trader reading a forecast of new investor supply may form a materially different risk expectation from one reading a community/Foundation-only installment, particularly when the headline total differs by almost ten million APT.

What could overturn the preliminary interpretation?

Tokenomics.com could identify a legitimate, distinct November tranche not covered by the original genesis schedule; publish a fully reconciled methodology showing why its current investor and insider labels remain appropriate; or demonstrate that the labels mean something broader than the original investor and contributor pools. Any of those findings would require revising the conclusion that its forecast appears to extend expired vesting entries.

Similarly, Aptos could publish account addresses and immutable lock controls that decisively validate its 210 million commitment. An independently reproduced historical transfer and resource audit could settle the timing of the last genesis investor distribution. At the time of this report, neither the final beneficiary-level transfer trail nor the permanent-lock enforcement had been established from the identified public records.

The Aptos Foundation has been sent a technical inquiry requesting custody addresses, transaction identifiers, withdrawal-authority information and an explanation of the November recipient categories. As of this research cutoff, no response is included in the record used for this article. A subsequent technical clarification would materially improve the supply accounting.

My conclusion: accurate supply reporting starts with accurate categories

What stands out to me is not merely the size of the conflicting numbers. It is how easily several true-sounding statements can become one misleading market narrative. Genesis vesting can be complete. Staking issuance can continue. A Foundation can promise to withhold principal. A tracker can still display a larger “unlock.” Each statement lives in a different accounting category.

The original Aptos schedule, the issuer’s completion statement and the independent smaller calendars all point in the same direction: routine genesis investor and contributor unlocks should no longer be appearing as November 2026 releases. The larger calendar’s investor and insider amounts resemble installments from that completed schedule, but its exact calculation method has not been established.

And the 210 million APT commitment remains a separate test of transparency. If the principal has been irreversibly locked, publishing the addresses and restrictions would let outside observers verify a meaningful reduction in available supply. Until then, the careful distinction is between a documented commitment and proven code-enforced permanence.

For anyone using unlock dashboards to understand supply risk, that distinction is the story.
