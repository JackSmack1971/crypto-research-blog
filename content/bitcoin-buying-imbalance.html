Bitcoin’s $1.2 Million Buying Imbalance—and the Reversal It Hasn’t Proven

On October 26, the crypto market looked calm on the surface. Beneath that calm, spot buyers and derivatives traders were telling a more complicated story.

October 26, 2026 · Analysis of observations from 09:00–12:45 UTC and earlier market snapshots

Bitcoin barely moved. That is the part of this story I keep returning to. A set of 6,233 reported BTC/USDT spot trades covering a little under four hours showed roughly $1.215 million more buyer-initiated than seller-initiated notional. Yet the first-to-last price change in that same record was only about 0.13%. The figures fit a narrative of accumulation. They also fit a market in which aggressive buyers are being met by substantial passive supply.

Those are not the same market. In one, demand is quietly building the base for a more durable advance. In the other, demand arrives but does surprisingly little to move the price. Until the distinction is resolved, I would resist calling the session a confirmed reversal.

The broader backdrop was restrained. An earlier OKX spot snapshot put Bitcoin near $80,356, ether around $2,326 and solana near $101.67. Across that snapshot’s rolling 24-hour comparison, BTC was down 0.12%, ETH down 0.40%, and SOL up 0.62%. These are historical intraday observations—not current quotes. They describe a market searching for a direction, not one that had found it.

Leverage was retreating. But not everywhere.

The first complication comes from the derivatives market. One global market-data feed reported crypto open interest down 2.64% over 24 hours and 14.7% over seven days. The same feed recorded approximately $35.73 million in BTC liquidations over the prior 24 hours, of which roughly 69% were long-side liquidations. Those numbers suggested leverage was being removed, especially from traders positioned for higher prices.

It is tempting to go directly from falling open interest to a bearish forecast. I think that skips the mechanism. An outstanding contract disappears when positions close; an OI decline does not, by itself, tell us whether distressed longs exited, shorts took profits, or both. Nor does a global figure describe every venue at every hour.

At Hyperliquid, a closer look at sixteen timestamped fifteen-minute observations from 09:00 through 12:45 UTC showed something narrower and more revealing. BTC open interest rose approximately 0.47%, from about 41,964 to 42,163 BTC, while its mark price edged up about 0.16%. In the same window, ETH open interest fell roughly 0.48%, from 1.107 million to 1.102 million ETH, while its mark was almost flat.

So the useful conclusion is not “crypto leverage is rising” or “crypto leverage is falling.” It is that exposure behaved differently by asset and venue, even while the broad market looked uneventful.

The spot tape adds weight—and ambiguity

Derivatives are only half the question. A price rise accompanied by increasing leveraged exposure can be fragile when the spot market is unwilling to absorb the flow. Conversely, a rise supported by sustained aggressive spot buying is more interesting, although even that cannot demonstrate who is ultimately holding the asset or for how long.

The BTC/USDT trade observations attribute 5,578 trades to Bitfinex and 655 to Kraken between 09:00 and 12:44:43 UTC. Across these records, buyer-initiated trades accounted for approximately 60.1% of notional, a positive signed imbalance of $1.215 million. The sign was positive in 11 of the 15 fifteen-minute buckets.

Here is why that finding matters: it is transaction-level evidence rather than merely an inference drawn from green candles. Each trade has a venue, timestamp, price, quantity and reported aggressor direction, making it possible to measure how much notional was initiated on either side of the spread.

But it is also easy to overread. Almost all of the net buying came from Bitfinex. Kraken’s aggregate imbalance was positive by only about $19,016, and its per-interval signs were mixed. The buying signal was not equally strong at both venues.

One fifteen-minute interval changes the picture

At 12:15 UTC, the recorded signed notional jumped by about $580,819. That single quarter-hour contributed nearly 48% of the entire window’s net buying imbalance. Remove it and the remaining intervals still show roughly $634,000 of positive signed flow. The positive sign survives. The appearance of a smoothly persistent campaign of buying does not.

I find that distinction more valuable than declaring a winner. A burst concentrated in one interval may reflect a news response, a large execution program, temporary liquidity conditions, or short-lived changes in trading activity. The observed flow does not establish intentional institutional accumulation.

A positive trade imbalance tells us who crossed the spread. It does not tell us whether the market has finished absorbing the supply waiting on the other side.

What the quiet price response is really saying

For perspective, the first and last BTC prices in the observed trading window were $80,231.00 and $80,333.30, a change of approximately 0.13%. An earlier market snapshot put Bitcoin’s rolling 24-hour range around $79,378 to $80,956. Against that backdrop, the strong positive reported signed flow produced relatively little net price movement.

There are at least two plausible readings. The constructive one is that willing buyers kept appearing without chasing price excessively, potentially forming a base. The skeptical one is that passive sellers were supplying enough BTC to absorb most of the aggressive buying, leaving the price vulnerable if demand fades. Both are compatible with the observed aggregates. Order-book replenishment, trade-by-trade impact and subsequent returns would help separate them.

Neither a positive CVD nor a rising open-interest line proves a durable edge. To claim predictive significance, I would want multiple comparable sessions, a pre-specified rule, realistic transaction costs and untouched out-of-sample results—not one visually compelling morning.

What would actually change my mind

For the bullish reading to deserve an upgrade, I would look for repeated positive spot-flow intervals across independently authenticated venues, increasing prices that hold their gains, and derivatives exposure that expands without extreme funding or deteriorating liquidity. A sustained move above the previously observed $80,956 rolling high would be more informative if accompanied by that evidence; the level itself is not a prediction.

For the bearish case, a loss of the earlier $79,378 range low alongside weakening spot flow would undermine the idea that buyers were establishing support. Rising derivatives OI against weakening price could also indicate new leveraged positioning in a deteriorating market, though it would still not identify the initiating side. Ether’s contrasting OI decline deserves attention precisely because a BTC-only story can hide weakening participation elsewhere.

The wider backdrop was not uniformly bullish. Different global-data providers disagreed materially on reported capitalization and BTC dominance: one estimated approximately $2.69 trillion in crypto market cap and 60.07% BTC dominance, while another estimated roughly $2.81 trillion and 57.44%, respectively. Those figures cannot responsibly be blended into a synthetic consensus without first reconciling methodology and coverage. A sentiment index showing 44 in one feed and 50 in another is similarly a reminder that an indicator is a product with a definition, not an absolute property of the market.

A market can be bought without being conquered

The October 26 tape has a real tension. The reported spot trades show a notable BTC buying imbalance. Hyperliquid BTC open interest grew while ether’s contracted. The broad derivatives market had already shed substantial exposure. And through all of it, Bitcoin scarcely advanced.

My interpretation is cautiously constructive about the presence of buying pressure, but agnostic about its durability. That is an important difference. The data make it harder to dismiss the market as nothing but leveraged speculation. They do not make it reasonable to declare a new upward trend.

The stronger question is not whether buyers showed up. In this sample, they did. The question is what happens when the next wave of buying stops—and whether price can hold without it.

Sources and methodology

Spot transactions: 6,233 reported BTC/USDT trades from Bitfinex and Kraken, October 26, 2026, 09:00:00–12:44:43 UTC. Signed notional is aggressor direction multiplied by price and BTC quantity.
