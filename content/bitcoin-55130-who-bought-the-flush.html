Bitcoin Rebounded at $55,130. The Tape Still Won’t Name the Buyer.

Binance and OKX show Bitcoin recovering while aggressive sellers remained in control of volume. The evidence supports absorption at the low—but not the identity or staying power of the buyers.

Planned publication: November 22, 2026 · Historical window: November 21–22, UTC · Not a live market update

At 06:17:15 UTC on November 21, Bitcoin traded near $55,130 on two major exchanges. The first temptation was to call the ensuing bounce a triumph of buyers. The trade records tell a less comfortable story: prices started climbing even though sellers were still the ones crossing the spread.

When I compare the signed spot executions on Binance and OKX with the liquidation record on Hyperliquid, the evidence is unusually specific. It gives me a defensible explanation for how the decline stopped accelerating. It does not give me a name for whoever was holding the bids, or proof that the same buyer kept the coins.

The moment selling stopped moving price the same way

The Binance BTCUSDT aggregate-trade archive places its observed low at 55,130 USDT at 06:17:15.845885 UTC. The independently processed OKX BTC-USDT tape reaches 55,134 USDT at 06:17:15.871 UTC. That is a nominal 25-millisecond difference in recorded timestamps—far too small, without clock and transport-latency controls, to declare either exchange the leader. Both prices are in USDT, not exchange-agnostic U.S. dollars. The data provenance is documented below.

Each exchange publishes trade direction in a way that makes a seller-initiated execution distinguishable from a buyer-initiated one. On Binance, the buyer-maker flag is inverted to determine the aggressor; on OKX, the recorded side is the taker side. That lets me calculate cumulative volume delta, or CVD: buyer-initiated BTC volume minus seller-initiated BTC volume. It is a measure of executed aggression, not a census of investors. [1] [2]

During the two minutes from 06:16 to 06:18 UTC, Binance recorded −140.94 BTC of net taker volume; OKX recorded −86.98 BTC. Both books printed their lows in that interval. More revealingly, in the next twelve minutes—the first leg of the recovery—Binance still registered −69.59 BTC and OKX −5.31 BTC. Prices rose anyway.

That is the observable divergence. It is consistent with buyers resting on the book absorbing market sells, with seller urgency easing, or with the location and quality of liquidity changing. It does not mean there were no aggressive buyers; it means their executed volume remained smaller than aggressive sellers' volume over those measured intervals.

The low was not defined by sellers disappearing. It was defined, at least on these two venues, by selling losing some of its ability to push price lower.

Only later did the order-flow balance turn positive. From 06:30 to 07:00 UTC, Binance logged roughly +47.71 BTC of CVD and OKX +100.24 BTC. The rebound acquired more buyer-initiated participation after the initial turn, not before it on a sustained interval basis.

The other side of the trade was under pressure

Spot order flow was not operating in isolation. In Hyperliquid's separate BTC perpetual market, a completed historical query for 06:10–06:25 UTC returned 1,022 deduplicated liquidation records: 1,008 long closures and 14 short closures. Between 06:16 and 06:18 alone, 870 long-liquidation records represented approximately 172.19 BTC and $9.51 million of execution notional. These are liquidation fills on one perpetual venue, not market-wide liquidations or equivalent spot sales. [3]

Hyperliquid's archived level-two snapshots add the missing physical constraint. The median quantity displayed across the first twenty bid levels fell from about 121.41 BTC at 06:00 to 39.90 BTC at 06:17. During the low minute, one snapshot contained only 2.22 BTC on those twenty bid levels, and the widest sampled spread was 17.55 basis points. Those numbers describe near-touch visible liquidity, not a constant-distance book-depth measure or a global market. [3]

I can call this a documented liquidity-fragility episode accompanied by forced-long closing. I cannot prove that the liquidations created the first selling pressure. The larger spot selloff had been running for hours, including sharply negative CVD on both exchanges between 03:30 and 04:15 UTC. Nor can I reconstruct a complete multi-exchange liquidation count from this one venue.

A rebound can last longer than the trade that supported it

The argument for absorption is stronger than a fleeting one-second anomaly. Reprocessing Binance's full UTC-day archive shows that from 06:18 to midnight, buyer-initiated volume totaled about 14,008.60 BTC, against 14,280.12 BTC of seller-initiated volume: a net −271.53 BTC. Over that span, the venue's spot price advanced from roughly 55,733 to 60,216 USDT. Its lowest subsequent print stayed above 55,719, rather than revisiting the 55,130 low.

That persistence matters. But it is not an account statement. A market maker can buy passively, hedge exposure in futures, replenish quotes and redistribute coins later. A long-horizon investor could leave almost the same visible footprint. CVD does not contain an account identifier, net inventory change, or the buyer's intended holding period.

The reported withdrawal of thousands of BTC by wallets attributed to Abraxas Capital is an intriguing competing explanation for broader accumulation. Those reports remain an attribution lead, not a trade-level match: I have not independently matched individual Bitcoin transfer hashes, exchange settlement timing, and wallet clustering to the specific resting orders at 06:17. Withdrawals from an exchange do not identify who made the best bid in its order book, and a withdrawal is not automatically a purchase. I will not give the anonymous buyer a famous name simply because the timing is suggestive.

The explanations that still survive

There are at least three coherent readings. One is genuine accumulation: participants with fresh risk appetite met the market's urgency and retained BTC. Another is temporary market-making: dealers warehouse exposure briefly, arbitrage cross-venue price differences, then neutralize the inventory elsewhere. A third is mostly mechanical relief: liquidations and urgent spot selling exhaust themselves, so prices recover even before aggressive demand takes over.

What would separate them is not another candle pattern. I would want authenticated maker-side order and execution records; exchange-attributed withdrawal transactions with timestamps and subsequent redeposit behavior; and synchronized spot, hedge and inventory changes over the following days. Continued positive taker flow across multiple spot venues and sustained higher lows would strengthen the durable-demand case. Renewed negative CVD accompanying a break under the low would weaken it. Maker-account records that showed rapid flatting or hedging would favor inventory recycling over long-term accumulation.

The public data cannot supply that ownership proof. Coinbase and Bitstamp were not included in the completed signed spot-tape comparison; OKX's signed series ends at 07:00 UTC. The full-day persistence test applies to Binance, not every market. And the distinction between BTC/USD and BTC/USDT matters even when nearby prices look similar.

The bid is real. The buyer is still a question.

I came looking for the hand behind Bitcoin's November 21 rebound. What the tapes reveal is more useful than a guessed identity: two exchanges recorded an almost simultaneous low, both initially recovered against net seller-initiated spot flow, and a separate perpetual venue recorded forced-long closing amid transiently depleted displayed bids.

That is a powerful explanation of how a falling market found a floor for the day. It is not proof of who owned the floor, how long they intended to hold it, or whether a lasting cycle bottom had been formed. Those are precisely the claims a dramatic chart cannot settle. The evidence has earned the word absorption. It has not yet earned the word accumulation.

Evidence and methods

All figures are historical as of November 22, 2026. Signed-volume deltas are computed in BTC from trades; negative CVD is net seller-initiated execution volume. Source rows and analytic handoffs are retained outside this publishable HTML. Trade-by-trade buyer identities and inventory records are not publicly supplied by the exchanges.
