Bitcoin at $76,000: The Monday Rebound That Still Needs Proof

Bitcoin stabilized near $76,000 after a sharp selloff, but ETF outflows, a misleadingly calm funding market and a rebound in open interest make one question unavoidable: is the selling finished, or merely paused?

Analysis dated 8 November 2026Market observations through approximately 12:07 UTC8-minute read

Bitcoin looked calmer on Sunday morning. That was the easy part to see. The harder part was deciding what the calm meant. After a rapid fall from above $80,000, the market was trading around $76,000, not far from lows that had already been tested. A quieter tape offered the outline of a bottom. It did not yet offer its proof.

At approximately 12:06 UTC on November 8, the OKX BTC/USDT spot price was 75,990.30 USDT. In the preceding days, price had briefly fallen to approximately 74,888 USDT. The distance between those observations matters less to me than the terrain between them. Buyers were defending part of the decline, but the market remained below its short-term averages. The apparent stability was arriving just as a difficult week of U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF redemptions ended.

My working view heading into Monday, November 9, is therefore cautious, not categorically bearish. Bitcoin has demonstrated the ability to stop falling for a time; it has not yet demonstrated the capacity to reclaim the territory lost during the selloff. The distinction is the entire investigation.

A selloff that changed the burden of proof

The decline was not a simple, orderly retreat. In the OKX exchange-session series, the session beginning November 3 closed near 80,300 USDT; the following session closed near 76,039 USDT, a drop of roughly 5.3%. Subsequent sessions stayed close to $76,000. A market can build a base after such a move, but the preceding break creates overhead supply: participants who would have preferred to sell higher can use the next rally as an exit.

That is why I care more about the quality of the next rebound than about its first few hundred dollars. The area around $77,000–$77,100 corresponds to a recent rebound high. Above it lies the seven-session average near $77,309. The twenty-session average was approximately $79,626. These are descriptions of historical structure, not walls where orders must be waiting.

A fourteen-session average true range of approximately $2,324—about 3.1% of the observed price—also argues against overconfidence in a narrow Monday forecast. ATR measures recent trading ranges, not a guaranteed future move. A market capable of covering that distance in an ordinary exchange session can invalidate a neat-looking thesis quickly.

Wall Street demand was sending a less reassuring message

Price alone can conceal the origin of demand. The week's ETF flows offered a different view. Reported U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF net flows for November 2–6 summed to approximately negative $1.282 billion. Four of the five sessions were negative. Friday, November 6, was reported at approximately negative $411.9 million.

These were sizeable figures, and I would not dismiss them as a footnote. They suggest that the most visible regulated U.S. access channel was seeing net redemptions during a period when Bitcoin needed additional demand. But the flow numbers require care: trackers did not fully agree. A competing Friday tally of roughly negative $8.9 million apparently omitted the approximately $403 million IBIT outflow present in the larger estimate. The discrepancy changes the daily conclusion materially, so the larger number should be read as a reported tally rather than an independently reconciled final accounting.

Nor is an ETF redemption automatically a same-minute Bitcoin sell order. Authorized-participant inventory, hedging and execution schedules complicate that transmission. What the figures can tell us is narrower and more useful: recent ETF demand was not an obvious source of support. What they cannot tell us is whether the next buyer at $75,000 will be a fund, a hedge, a market maker or somebody entirely different.

The leverage market was not screaming panic

There is a familiar shortcut in crypto commentary: rising open interest means bulls are piling in; falling open interest means bears are winning. Neither statement survives basic mechanics. Every open long has a matching short. Open interest counts outstanding contracts, not a net vote on price direction.

On Hyperliquid, the BTC perpetual's hourly open-interest series fell from approximately 42,244 BTC at 00:00 UTC on November 7 to 41,563 BTC at 00:00 UTC on November 8, then rose to approximately 42,107 BTC by noon Sunday. That last twelve-hour increase—about 1.31%—is important, but it is not directional by itself. It shows gross exposure returning as price stabilized, not whether the returning risk is fundamentally long or short.

The funding market was similarly subdued. On OKX, eleven of the twelve most recent BTC perpetual settlements were positive, yet the latest settled rate was only approximately 0.00336% per eight-hour settlement. Hyperliquid's sampled hourly rates were roughly 0.00125% per hour. The two contracts have different settlement clocks; comparing their raw percentages without adjusting the interval would mislead. Neither series, taken on its own, demonstrates dangerously one-sided positioning.

The uncomfortable possibility is that leverage is returning before demand has convincingly returned.

That is a risk hypothesis, not a verdict. A squeeze could follow if shorts are trapped; a fresh liquidation wave could follow if new longs are weakly margined. To distinguish those outcomes would require matched price, changes in contract counts, signed trading activity and liquidation evidence across a meaningful interval—not simply a funding print and an OI snapshot.

What forty seconds of spot trading can—and cannot—say

The most revealing small test was also the easiest to overinterpret. A window of 100 OKX BTC/USDT trades between 12:06:42 and 12:07:20 UTC on Sunday recorded approximately 0.3343 BTC marked buy-side and 0.4373 BTC marked sell-side. Based on the reported side labels, sellers exceeded buyers by 0.1030 BTC; buys accounted for 43.3% of the sampled volume.

That is a real imbalance in the sample, not evidence that the entire market was under sustained selling pressure. The time window was less than 40 seconds, the volume was tiny relative to the broader BTC market, and the venue's trade-side labels were not independently reconciled against simultaneous quotes. The companion sample of 100 OKX BTC perpetual trades was closer to balanced: approximately 61.15 BTC buy-side and 64.45 BTC sell-side, a signed volume imbalance of roughly −2.6%.

If the thesis is that heavy selling continues on Monday, those samples do not establish it. I would rather acknowledge the limit than convert a convenient burst of transactions into a predictive story.

Monday has two different tests

The first is a price test. A sustained reclaim of $77,100 would challenge the immediate bearish reading, particularly if the market then holds above the roughly $77,309 seven-session average. A sustained break below $75,300, followed by a failure at the earlier low near $74,888, would make downside continuation more plausible. The operative word is sustained: a brief wick through a level has far less evidentiary value than repeated acceptance and corroborating activity.

The second is a participation test. Monday's U.S. session needs to show whether buying and selling actually expand around those levels; whether open interest rises because new exposure is opening rather than merely moving with the mark price; and whether the day's ETF activity reverses the preceding week's redemptions. A price bounce without better participation can still happen, but it earns less interpretive weight.

Macro timing adds another complication. Monday precedes the scheduled U.S. October CPI release on Tuesday, November 10, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Treasury auctions and policy commentary can affect broader risk appetite, but I would not claim a single scheduled event has already dictated Bitcoin's next move. The more defensible point is that participants will be entering an important inflation release with price structure already damaged.

The countercase deserves serious attention

There is a credible bullish explanation for everything that looks weak. Large ETF redemptions may have been absorbed. The market recovered from a low under $75,000. Funding was not euphoric, so a reflexive crowded-long unwinding argument has less force. Open interest returning during stabilization could reflect hedges, short covering or outright risk-taking that ultimately supports a recovery. And an oversold market does not require a beautiful macro backdrop to stage a forceful relief rally.

The bearish countercase is equally straightforward. A temporary base below two short-term moving averages can become a pause before another leg down. Renewed leverage without durable spot demand increases sensitivity to bad news. If the market cannot reclaim its nearby rebound high while ETF demand remains negative, the path back toward the prior low becomes easier to imagine. Neither interpretation earns an automatic victory merely because it fits the last price move.

I am deliberately not assigning numerical odds to those paths. Doing so without a calibrated forecast model, a comparable historical sample and tested error rates would give the appearance of precision without its substance.

What the $76,000 stalemate really reveals

Bitcoin's next move will be narrated afterward as though it were obvious. For now the interesting fact is the mismatch between what the market makes visible and what it conceals. We can observe price, selected open-interest histories and reported ETF flows. We cannot infer the identities, conviction or constraints of the marginal participants from those measurements alone. We also cannot assume one perpetual venue represents the global derivatives market.

That is why I regard $76,000 less as an answer than as a stress point for competing explanations. If buyers reclaim the lost range with sustained participation, the market will have supplied evidence for a bottoming process. If the nearby lows fail while risk is being rebuilt, it will have supplied evidence that the calm was merely an interval between selling waves.

The lesson is not to forecast nothing. It is to demand that a directional thesis survive contact with the next observation. Bitcoin has stabilized. Whether it has recovered remains an open question.
