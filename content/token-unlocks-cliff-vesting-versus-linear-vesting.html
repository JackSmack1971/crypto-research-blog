Token Unlocks: Why a Cliff Is Not the Same Supply Shock as Linear Vesting

Arbitrum's original vesting disclosures put more than 1.1 billion insider tokens on a single modeled cliff, while a separate Foundation allocation accrued continuously. The timing difference is measurable; the selling pressure is not.

Research checked November 22, 2026 (America/Detroit) · Historical documents dated March 2024 · ARB units throughout · No live-price or trading-flow claim

On March 7, 2024, Arbitrum's own supply table estimated 1,537,398,145 ARB in circulation. Its projection for March 17 was 2,653,939,384. That apparent leap of more than 1.1 billion tokens was mostly scheduled insider vesting—not evidence of 1.1 billion tokens hitting exchange order books. The difference matters: a calendar can tell me when a grant is due to become available. It cannot tell me who sold it. [1]

I wanted to isolate the part of the story that the schedule can establish. A cliff withholds an initial grant and releases a large tranche on a stated date. Linear vesting accrues steadily. They can ultimately make the same number of tokens eligible, but their release patterns are not the same. Arbitrum's original team-and-investor schedule and its separate Foundation arrangement make that distinction unusually measurable. [2]

The finding: The disclosed team-and-investor allocation yields a modeled 1,111,750,000-ARB first cliff, followed by roughly 92,645,833 ARB per monthly installment. The first cliff is exactly twelve such tranches. Neither number measures sales.

What the schedule actually promises

Arbitrum's historical allocation sets aside 2.694 billion ARB for team and contributors and 1.753 billion for investors: 4.447 billion combined. Its supply explanation says the four-year term began March 16, 2023, with the first unlock on March 16, 2024 and monthly unlocks thereafter. The issuer's March 17 projection puts 673.5 million team tokens and 438.25 million investor tokens in circulation—one-quarter of each allocation. [1] [2]

I model the remaining three-quarters as 36 equal post-cliff monthly tranches. This follows the described four-year term and cadence; it does not reconstruct every token-level claim, contract rounding rule, or intraday release instant. The first modeled month is March 16, 2024 at 00:00 UTC for chart alignment only. The primary documents specify the calendar date, not this precise release hour.

The Foundation comparison works differently. Arbitrum's historical accounting assigns 750 million ARB to the Foundation, including 50 million initially distributed and 700 million in the vesting budget. The latter begins vesting April 17, 2023 over four years, with the issuer describing per-second accrual. An entitlement accruing each second is not proof that someone withdraws, transfers, or sells tokens each second. [1]

One date overwhelms eleven ordinary months

The larger concentration is mechanical, not a market forecast. Twenty-five percent of 4.447 billion is 1.11175 billion. Seventy-five percent divided into 36 installments is 92.645833 million each. Divide the first by the second and the answer is exactly twelve. In other words, the first insider cliff is a year's worth of the later modeled monthly pace delivered at one point in the calendar.

The Foundation's monthly accrual varies slightly because months have different numbers of days; those differences are visible even with constant per-second vesting. I used calendar dates in UTC and preserved the actual leap-day interval in the four-year denominator. A tracker that simply assumes a uniform 30-day month can introduce avoidable distortions.

The denominator trap: 72.3 percent of what?

The first cliff divided by Arbitrum's own March 7, 2024 estimated circulating base equals 72.3137%. A later ordinary monthly insider tranche equals 6.0261% of that same fixed historical base. This is a consistent size comparison; it is not a claim that circulating supply grew by 72.3%, nor a statement about what was freely tradable on March 16 or in November 2026.

Arbitrum's own documentation notes that its supply estimate may differ from actual circulation, including because of DAO treasury distribution or vesting-contract behavior. Its accounting also includes an airdrop category holding certain unclaimed tokens returned to treasury while preserving presentation consistency. I therefore cannot treat this denominator as a verified order-book float. [1]

Precision is not independent verification

The strongest check I can perform with the issuer's historical tables is arithmetic. If I allocate the 700 million Foundation vesting principal uniformly over the actual seconds from April 17, 2023 to April 17, 2027, then add the 50 million initially distributed, I obtain 205,715,263.518 ARB for March 7, 2024 and 210,506,502.396 for March 17. Rounded to whole tokens, they reproduce the official Foundation entries of 205,715,264 and 210,506,502. [1]

There is even a tiny disclosed-table wrinkle worth retaining. Arbitrum's headline total changes by 1,116,541,239 tokens between its March 7 estimate and March 17 projection, while the sum of the changed component rows is 1,116,541,238. The issuer warns that values are rounded. That one-token discrepancy is not evidence of economically meaningful leakage; it is a reason to state the precision of what I have reconstructed.

At the same time, I have not reconstructed historical withdrawals from the chain. The official deployment list identifies the Arbitrum One ARB contract as 0x912CE59144191C1204E64559FE8253a0e49E6548 and the Foundation vesting budget address as 0x15533b77981cDa0F85c4F9a485237DF4285D6844. That gives a route for independent verification, not completed verification of historical payouts. [3]

The strongest counterargument to cliff panic

A token becoming eligible to move does not mean its owner rushes to sell. Vesting can be anticipated; holders may retain balances, delegate governance weight, arrange custody or transact later. Conversely, a smooth accrual schedule need not create smooth sell flow: a recipient can wait and sell multiple accrued months together. I find no defensible way to convert a release schedule into an estimated sell ratio without wallet-level and venue-level evidence.

To test a price-impact thesis, I would first reconcile vesting contract state with transfers and actual token availability; then separate recipient-controlled transfers from exchange-linked deposits; then compare matched spot returns and executable liquidity across venues around the event. Even an exchange deposit is not necessarily an executed sale. The dataset here contains none of that transaction-level market evidence. It establishes a timing mechanism, not a short signal.

What would change the conclusion?

The accounting would need revision if governance changed grant conditions, if the exact month or second rules differed from the documentary approximation, or if chain-state reconstruction contradicted the reported entitlement. A measured market effect would require observed transfers, ownership attribution with stated limits, dated spot-flow data and an appropriate comparison against market-wide moves. A controlled sample of several independent cliff and linear events would be necessary before claiming one mechanism predictably depresses prices more than the other.

For now I would use a large dated cliff as a prompt to investigate who can actually sell and whether buyers could absorb it, not as a proxy for sales. The calendar tells us how inventory could become available. The order book, if we can observe it, tells us something very different.

Questions readers should ask

Does a token unlock guarantee a sale?

No. Vesting eligibility, token withdrawal, transfers, exchange deposits, and executions are separate events.

Is the cliff larger than the historical float?

In this example the first cliff was about 72.3% of the issuer's earlier estimated circulating base, not greater than 100% and not proof of that percentage reaching the market.

Why can a linear schedule still create sudden selling?

Recipients need not sell when tokens accrue. They can accumulate transferable balances and sell in batches.
