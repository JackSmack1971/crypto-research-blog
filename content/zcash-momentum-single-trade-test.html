Zcash’s Momentum Test Turned on a Single Trade

A November 18 screen of ten liquid crypto pairs found relative strength inside a broad selloff. A historical simulation exposed why none qualified for a trade.

November 18, 2026 · Historical market investigation · All quotations are time-specific

Three simulated Zcash trades turned a small, deliberately constrained allocation into a positive result. Remove the contribution of just one of them, and the profit becomes a loss. That is the most revealing detail in a November 18 investigation of ten relatively liquid cryptocurrency pairs: the strongest-looking candidate did not fail because it lacked a convincing price story. It failed because the story depended on an event that had not been shown to repeat.

Zcash had gained 62.54% over 90 days while Bitcoin had lost 12.90%. Yet both were down sharply over the preceding month. Hyperliquid, another relative outperformer, offered a different economic narrative, tied to protocol fees and token supply. XRP had held up better than Bitcoin over the latest week, suggesting a possible reversal. There was more than enough here to generate a watchlist. There was not enough to turn it into a dependable strategy.

My conclusion: the evidence favors further research on ZEC, not a trade. Relative strength inside a selloff is a legitimate observation; repeatable net profitability is a separate, considerably harder claim.

Ten pairs, all down

The daily spot evidence was unusually consistent. At the close of the 24-hour trading interval ending November 18 at 16:00 UTC, every one of the ten screened OKX USDT pairs had declined over both seven and 30 days. All ten were below their 20-, 50- and 100-day simple moving averages. A separate initial twelve-asset panel was also below its 20- and 50-day averages. That is broad weakness within the examined groups, not a census of the entire crypto market. Bitcoin’s turnover over the latest seven days was 54% higher than in the preceding thirty-day period, a sign of exceptional participation in a falling market—not proof that sellers had exhausted themselves.

The investable-looking group was deliberately narrow. The discovery screen began with a partial review of CoinPaprika’s top 60, excluding dollar-linked and duplicate wrapped exposures, then looked for at least $1 billion in market capitalization, $25 million in reported global daily volume, ninety completed daily bars, median OKX quote turnover of 10 million USDT, a quoted spread no greater than ten basis points, and at least 100,000 USDT of displayed depth per side within fifty basis points of the midpoint. Ten pairs met the provisional thresholds. These criteria were exploratory and adjusted after initial observations; ten names in the larger screen were not fully qualified. Some major assets failed the specific venue-turnover test, not necessarily a test of liquidity elsewhere.

ZEC and HYPE were the only nonbenchmark candidates to outperform Bitcoin over all three measured horizons. XRP’s comparative advantage was shorter-lived; it outperformed BTC by 5.75 percentage points over seven days but trailed it over thirty and ninety. NEAR was more extreme: a 90-day gain of nearly 85% sat beside a 34.58% monthly loss. PEPE’s 30-day loss was the smallest of the group, but its seven-day decline exceeded 25%. The leader depends on the clock and the horizon.

That last observation matters because a ranking can introduce its own distortion. A signed negative return divided by volatility becomes less negative as volatility rises. With identical −20% returns, the quotient is −0.50 at 40% volatility but −0.20 at 100% volatility. If a high quotient attracts a higher score, the riskier loser gets rewarded. The discovery ranking contained that weakness, as well as an identical missing-evidence penalty for assets whose structural risks were hardly identical. I would retire that score as a predictive signal rather than give its ordering undue authority.

The clock that almost manufactured a price discrepancy

Before attaching significance to the relative returns, a seemingly serious disagreement had to be understood. The November 17 bar-opening date in the exchange’s native daily series showed Bitcoin closing at 63,123.60 USDT. Massive’s November 17 BTC/USD daily close was $64,873.18. A difference of about 2.77% is large enough to question the comparison.

But those bars did not end at the same moment. OKX’s native day opened at 16:00 UTC; Massive’s daily aggregation began at midnight. Reconstructing the November 17 UTC day from twenty-four completed OKX hourly candles gave a 64,947.80 USDT close, only about 0.115% above the Massive dollar reference. Four other pairs showed similarly small discrepancies. The USD and USDT units are still distinct, and only this day was independently reconciled.

Zcash was resilient—until the market’s worst days

ZEC is the interesting case precisely because it does not fit the simplest bearish narrative. Its 90-day performance was substantial, and its seven- and thirty-day returns were less negative than Bitcoin’s. Controlling for a simple estimated relationship with Bitcoin also left positive relative performance. The question was whether that made Zcash meaningfully safer or more predictive.

A regression on the first 78 daily log returns estimated each asset’s sensitivity to BTC. Applying that fixed coefficient to the final 30 returns gave ZEC about +17.72 log-percentage points after subtracting the estimated Bitcoin exposure. XRP showed +14.41 and HYPE +13.33. But PEPE showed +47.86 under the same diagnostic. That last result is a useful reminder: removing one market factor is not the same as discovering alpha. A BTC-only regression omits other exposures, and the final thirty days had already informed candidate selection.

My interpretation is that Zcash’s relative-momentum case is worth investigating, but its behavior under severe stress rules out treating it as a demonstrated defensive asset. When the market is losing liquidity, relative resilience measured over ordinary days may not be the kind of protection an investor actually needs.

Relative strength did not reliably persist

A forward-return diagnostic was deliberately simpler than any trading strategy. At eleven historical decision dates, spaced seven days apart, an asset qualified when both its preceding seven- and thirty-day log returns beat Bitcoin. The outcome was its next seven-day log return minus BTC’s. The outcome intervals did not overlap, although the lookback windows did. That design reduces one obvious dependence problem without claiming independence or statistical significance.

NEAR is inconvenient evidence in the best sense: it exposes how much the result depends on a shortlist assembled after looking at the market. A favorable diagnostic for ZEC is not meaningless, but a stronger-looking rival should not be quietly removed from consideration. HYPE’s negative result is equally important. It attacks the persistence mechanism itself. A legitimate hypothesis cannot be rescued merely by changing the thresholds until the historical numbers improve.

Token structure is part of the trade

Price analysis also needs to survive contact with the assets’ economic design. Hyperliquid is especially instructive. CoinGecko’s November 18, 18:58:58 UTC supply snapshot reported about 237.71 million HYPE circulating against 949.84 million total, or 25.03%. Issuer disclosures describe a genesis allocation of one billion units: 38.89% for future emissions and rewards, 31% for genesis distribution, 23.8% for core contributors, 6% for the Foundation and roughly 0.31% for grants and liquidity programs. Contributor vesting commenced in November 2025, with schedules described as extending into 2027–28. These are allocation and schedule descriptions, not independently reconciled sales or a precise next unlock.

An issuer filing and a second issuer disclosure describe the long-term supply framework; Hyperliquid’s fee documentation describes fee-funded acquisitions and token removal. Economic activity could support demand. Conversely, new circulating supply could overwhelm the effect. Without measuring actual burns, net emissions and distributions on comparable dates, neither side of that argument is established. The original genesis allocations are not measures of current ownership concentration.

XRP presents a different kind of supply question. Ripple’s Q1 2025 XRP Markets Report distinguished tokens held directly from escrowed XRP. Releases concern tokens that already existed, rather than fresh minting. Gross releases, net re-escrow and actual distribution to the market are three different quantities. CoinGecko reported 63.52 billion XRP circulating out of 99.99 billion total; the residual is not a verified Ripple balance. Native XRP is also distinct from issued assets subject to XRPL freeze rules. The original short-term resilience observation tells us nothing conclusive about whether large holders were accumulating or distributing.

Zcash’s monetary limit of 21 million coins is described in project documentation, and the upcoming changes add protocol-specific uncertainty to its price behavior. The Zcash Foundation published NU7 polling results on September 14, Zebra 7.0.0 on October 22 and Zebra 7.1.0 security fixes on November 16. The previously announced activation target—around November 5 at height 3,543,405—was a planned event, not an established activation timestamp in this research. A subsequently announced NU7.1 advisory poll deadline was December 1 at 02:59 UTC. These developments may matter to the market, but no abnormal-return event study tied them causally to price. Shielded balances also prevent transparent-address statistics from establishing total ownership concentration.

Governance is another place where a screening statistic can mislead. Ethereum token interfaces for UNI and PEPE were identified at the canonical mainnet contracts. UNI exposed mint and setMinter; PEPE exposed blacklist, setRule and ownership-management functions. Those interfaces do not establish who can currently exercise them. Current ownership, minter state, proxy status and concentrations were not reconciled to a finalized block. I would distinguish a demonstrated interface from a demonstrated live privilege. UNI’s minting framework also contradicts treating a reported one-billion maximum as a permanent cap. A fee-to-burn governance mechanism may be interesting, but gross fees, protocol revenue and actual burns are not interchangeable.

Relevant evidence includes the UNI Ethereum contract, PEPE Ethereum contract, UNI published source code and UNIfication governance record. Native BTC has a declining issuance schedule; ETH has dynamic issuance and fee burning; SOL has an inflation schedule and validator dependencies; DOGE continues issuance. No comprehensive holder census or contemporaneous incident audit was established for the group, and NEAR’s current governance and issuance mechanics were not fully qualified.

Leverage and liquidity complicate the chart

Funding does not resolve the momentum question. On OKX, the twelve realized eight-hour funding settlements between November 15 and 18 averaged positive for BTC, ZEC and HYPE, negative for ETH and XRP. Liquid’s venue-specific positioning comparison classified the five markets as balanced. Its selected-cohort long shares ranged from 42% to 46%, with reported positive funding, but its precise observation timestamp and funding interval were not established. The open-interest fields should not be combined across venues without confirming contract definitions. Changes between two short-spaced OKX observations were within about 0.08% in underlying units: not enough to make a confident story about multi-day leverage expansion. Historical basis, liquidation and spot-flow evidence did not establish a causal mechanism.

The experiment that put the narrative under pressure

The historical simulation addressed a more difficult question than whether relative strength can be measured. Could that condition support a simple, repeatable rule after costs? Two long-only spot strategies were specified from the earlier research. ZEC qualified when its preceding seven- and thirty-day log returns both exceeded BTC’s. XRP qualified when Bitcoin’s 30-day return and XRP’s seven-day return were negative, but XRP had fallen less than BTC over seven days. Only completed bars were used. Decisions were made every seven days, with entry modeled at the observed opening price 24 hours later and exit seven days after entry. Those are modeled fills, not observed transactions.

The test used 109 completed native-clock bars for BTC, ZEC and XRP, with ten weekly evaluation opportunities over a 70-day interval. The hypothetical capital base was 40,000 USDT—not an actual account—and each allocation was capped at 2.5% of current simulated equity, 1,000 USDT, and 0.01% of trailing 30-day median turnover. The full turnover gate was ten million USDT. Cash earned nothing; leverage, funding and borrowing were absent. The base cost model charged 25 bps per side (10 fee, 2.5 half-spread, 12.5 slippage/impact), with nominal round-trip sensitivities of 25, 50 and 100 bps.

These assumptions create a reproducible exploratory simulation; they do not reconstruct actual fees, available historical depth, nonfills or the precise costs of trading in stressed conditions. The simple ZEC comparator omitted the 30-day condition. The XRP comparator omitted the relative-strength filter. Passive exposure and cash were additional baselines. The passive rule’s fixed allocation did not separately apply the weekly turnover eligibility screen, a small but real asymmetry.

ZEC’s dual condition ended the experiment at a positive 1.134% net hypothetical NAV return after base costs. Its simpler seven-day version returned 1.046%. That incremental difference, 0.088 percentage points, is not large enough to establish that the added filter contributes repeatable information. XRP’s resilience rule and the simple dip rule selected exactly the same two trades; both lost 0.479% of modeled NAV. Cash beat them. The relative filter contributed no observable improvement.

When a complicated signal selects the same events as a simpler one, the simplest explanation is not that the complex signal has hidden power. It is that, in this sample, its extra condition did no work. Larger independent samples could tell a different story, but the result here offers no reason to promote it.

Risk budgets are not loss guarantees

A mechanical risk rule can help describe a scenario without turning the strategy into a recommendation. For ZEC, a November 18 snapshot put the midpoint at 910.695 USDT; the simple 14-day average true range was 108.43 USDT, or about 11.68% of the last completed daily close. A proposed stop-distance model of 1.5 ATR, plus a 5% gap allowance relative to the entry reference and 100 bps nominal round-trip friction, implied a loss of approximately 216.25 USDT per unit at that stressed exit. The model is hypothetical. Neither the stop overlay nor a proposed veto against negative BTC trends was historically tested.

The distinction between monetary risk and committed capital can be expressed without assuming anyone’s actual holdings. The modeled loss per unit is the entry cost inclusive of friction minus the proceeds from the stressed exit, also net of friction. Divide a hypothetical risk budget by that loss; cap the result by allocation, turnover and displayed-depth participation. A gap can be much larger than the modeled allowance; custody failure and USDT depeg introduce separate exposures. A printed stop reference is not a promised execution price.

Model: L = P(1 + c) − max(0, P − m × ATR − g × P)(1 − c); units = min(B/L, cap/[P(1+c)]). P = reference price; c = proportional cost per side; m = ATR multiple; g = gap allowance; B = hypothetical risk budget. Cap is the lesser of 2.5% hypothetical NAV, 1,000 USDT, 0.01% median turnover and 1% of observed minimum-side depth.

What would actually justify changing the verdict?

I would not advance the ZEC hypothesis to an approved paper allocation. The latest completed relative-strength conditions were satisfied, but the BTC 30-day return was negative. The visible liquidity snapshot was bounded, current protocol-event qualification was incomplete, and neither the predictive edge nor the new risk overlay had been independently tested. A weekly prospective observation—logging signals, hypothetical nonfills, subsequent outcomes and invalidations—could be useful without taking or pretending to take a position.

Zcash remains the main research question; XRP’s incremental resilience thesis is contradicted within the specific simulated experiment, and HYPE’s original momentum hypothesis is weakened enough to suspend it. NEAR remains an important competing explanation for any success attributed specifically to ZEC. PEPE’s adverse short-horizon evidence and unresolved structural risks argue against promotion; DOGE lacks a compelling surviving thesis; BTC and ETH remain controls, while SOL and UNI require their own independently defined research questions.

To change the result, the dual-horizon signal would need to demonstrate incremental, net performance beyond the simple seven-day rule on genuinely unseen observations, in a historical universe defined as it existed at each date. The next experiment should seek at least 730 completed daily observations where available, check independently sourced prices, preserve historical listings and delistings, predefine rules and competing hypotheses, and separate discovery, calibration, validation and an untouched holdout. Label-overlap purging and an embargo, dependence-aware uncertainty estimates, and multiple-testing controls belong in that design. The acquisition target alone does not guarantee statistical power. Execution costs, depth and operational incidents should be evaluated on their own evidence rather than silently inferred from aggregate turnover.

That is the central tension in crypto research. The market can offer vivid divergences, extraordinary returns and plausible economic stories. The uncomfortable work is deciding which of those survive the simplest objections. What stands out in this investigation is not that no trade emerged. It is how close the most promising thesis came to looking convincing before one unusually successful observation was put in context. A market signal that cannot survive that question is still a research question.
