RUNE’s Quiet Repricing Problem

THORChain is still moving meaningful value across chains. Its token is a fraction of its former price. The gap is intriguing—but the case for a recovery is more complicated than either number suggests.

October 22, 2026Research perspective: 7–30 days and 3–12 monthsFigures are dated observations, not live quotes

A token can lose almost all of its market value without its underlying network disappearing. That is the tension I see in THORChain’s RUNE. As of October 22, 2026, RUNE was trading near $0.68, about 97% below its 2021 peak, while THORChain continued to process substantial cross-chain swap activity. The contrast invites an obvious question: has the market overcorrected, or is the low price telling us something that protocol usage alone cannot capture?

I lean toward a narrower, less dramatic answer. RUNE may be undervalued relative to a functioning cross-chain network, but there is not yet enough evidence to call it a confirmed recovery trade. The supply structure has improved. The product has utility. The fee stream is real. Yet normalized demand, tokenholder value capture, operational reliability and current positioning all complicate the bullish narrative.

The case begins with a real product, not a ticker

THORChain occupies a specific part of crypto infrastructure: native-asset swaps between blockchains, without relying on conventional wrapped-asset bridges for the core exchange proposition. That is a useful distinction. Crypto has more than enough speculative assets whose supposed product-market fit exists mostly in a slide deck. THORChain’s demand can at least be examined through swap volume, fee generation, bonded security and active users.

Its September 2026 network report put monthly swap volume at approximately $2.40 billion and system income at $3.01 million. Active wallets reportedly climbed to roughly 25,000, the third consecutive monthly increase. On the surface, that is a respectable activity profile for a token capitalized near $226 million.

But I would not annualize those September figures and declare victory. The same report attributes approximately $1.37 billion of the month’s volume to the September 25–29 window, associated with funds linked to the Bitget exploit. That means more than half the headline monthly volume occurred in one unusual period. It may represent genuine exchange activity, but it is not reliable evidence of repeatable, ordinary customer demand.

This is the first test any serious valuation argument must survive: what does THORChain look like after the exceptional flow is separated from the recurring business? A platform can handle an extraordinary burst of volume and still have a weaker underlying trend than the monthly total suggests. The distinction matters because persistent activity, not a single episode, is what could justify a durable token rerating.

The market may be discounting a working protocol. But a working protocol is not the same thing as a token with strong, direct value capture.The central valuation distinction

Cleaner supply does not mean simple token economics

RUNE’s supply profile is more attractive than the dilution-heavy models common across crypto. CoinGecko’s October 22 reference figures indicated about 328.5 million tokens circulating and a circulating valuation near 93% of fully diluted valuation. THORChain’s own token information states that the final scheduled unlock occurred in 2023. Future scheduled unlock pressure is therefore not the dominant issue it is for some newer projects.

That is meaningful, but it only removes one source of risk. It does not answer whether economic activity benefits the token in proportion to its market price. Network revenue and tokenholder cash flows are not interchangeable.

THORChain’s income-distribution framework directs approximately 59% of system income to nodes and pools, 20% to protocol-owned liquidity, 10% to TCY, 5% each to developers and marketing, and 1% to RUNE burn. The direct burn allocation was reduced from 5% to 1% as protocol-owned liquidity assumed a larger role.

There is a coherent design argument behind this. Liquidity held or managed at the protocol level may improve resilience and the network’s economics over time. Yet that potential benefit is indirect. A dollar of swap fees is not a dollar paid to a RUNE holder, and a low market-cap-to-fees ratio cannot be interpreted as if RUNE were an ordinary equity claim on the network’s revenues.

My interpretation is that the asset has become less of an aggressive burn story and more of a security-and-liquidity coordination story. That may be sustainable, but it is harder to value. I would rather see a defensible model of fee retention, bonded RUNE, protocol-owned liquidity and actual token demand than a neat multiple that pretends those channels are equivalent.

The chart is not confirming the fundamental story yet

The October 22 Hyperliquid RUNE reference was near $0.678. A contemporaneous hourly indicator snapshot showed RSI around 50.6: neither deeply oversold nor especially strong. Price was above the 50-hour moving average near $0.676 and the 200-hour moving average near $0.650, but below the 20-hour average near $0.683 and the preceding 24-hour volume-weighted average near $0.690. The MACD histogram was slightly negative, and on-balance volume had been falling over the prior 20 hourly observations.

None of this establishes a statistically reliable forecast. It does, however, describe the current tension precisely: a medium-term recovery structure paired with short-term hesitation. I see neither a clean breakdown nor convincing evidence that buyers are expanding their participation.

Perpetual derivatives tell a similarly mixed story. The Hyperliquid/Liquid snapshot showed approximately $2.47 million in RUNE open interest and a small positive funding figure of about 0.0013% per funding interval. The sampled profitable-trader cohort was approximately 45% long, compared with about 61% long among the sampled losing-trader cohort. That difference is worth monitoring, but it is venue-specific and classification-dependent. It is not proof that informed traders expect a decline.

Nor does open interest resolve who is in control. Every outstanding long has a corresponding short. OI describes outstanding exposure; it does not independently tell us whether aggressive new risk-taking will push prices higher or lower.

A caution hidden in the order book

There is another reason I am reluctant to infer too much precision from this market. A short historical reconstruction of Hyperliquid’s RUNE order book covered approximately 58 seconds around October 19–20. It processed 2,074 recorded L4 events and found no orphan removals, orphan updates or reversed event order in the returned sequence. That is a useful preliminary integrity check.

But the rebuilt top of book did not agree with a separately archived L2 sample. The replay showed a best bid and ask of $0.64650 and $0.64666; the archived L2 sample showed $0.64654 and $0.64685. Not one of the first 20 price levels on either side matched exactly when price, quantity and order count were considered. Differences in checkpoint cursors or sample-time semantics may explain the divergence, but the cause was not established.

This is not proof that THORChain liquidity is defective, nor that the market is manipulated. It is a narrower research warning: an apparently complete event replay is not automatically a reliable execution model. Until archival semantics and trade-to-order reconciliation are resolved, claims about historical queue priority, fill probability or precise slippage from that data would be overstated. For an illiquid or latency-sensitive strategy, false precision can be more dangerous than openly acknowledging missing data.

The operational risk belongs in the valuation

THORChain’s product is technically demanding. Native cross-chain settlement depends on signing, coordination, network security and reliable execution. The project’s September 2026 development communication emphasized stability and work on TSS signing issues before expanding to additional chains.

I read that as sensible prioritization, not automatically as a sign of failure. Still, reliability is central to the investment thesis. Every technical interruption or security incident can influence participation, fee generation and the premium investors are willing to assign to the token. The bull case cannot reasonably celebrate cross-chain utility while treating operational risk as an afterthought.

What would actually change my view?

Over the next several weeks, a sustained recovery through approximately $0.69–$0.70 alongside stronger spot participation would improve the near-term picture. Continued weakness toward the recent $0.63–$0.65 area would instead be consistent with the market discount remaining in place. These are reference areas drawn from the October 22 price structure, not validated entry, stop or target levels. There are no calibrated probabilities attached to them.

Over three to twelve months, the more decisive evidence would be fundamental: recurring swap volume after removing unusual flow episodes; income that remains healthy across different volatility regimes; growth in sustainably bonded RUNE; improved network reliability; and a clearer connection between protocol-owned liquidity and demand for the token. I would also watch whether direct burns and broader economic incentives produce measurable benefits rather than simply more complicated accounting.

The opposing scenario is straightforward. Normalized swap activity could stagnate, rival routing and swap infrastructure could capture more demand, or another operational problem could undermine confidence. Under those conditions, a 97% drawdown would not, by itself, make RUNE inexpensive. Assets can remain far below former peaks because their prospective economics have changed.

Why this matters beyond one token

RUNE illustrates a wider problem in crypto valuation. A protocol can generate real fees, secure real assets and perform a useful service, yet the token representing its economic coordination layer may capture those benefits imperfectly. Traditional revenue multiples become seductive precisely when they are least appropriate.

The better question is not simply whether a network is being used. It is how the value generated by that usage moves through the system, who bears the risks, who receives the cash flows or other benefits, and whether the market price adequately reflects those relationships. In that sense, RUNE is an unusually revealing case study: the product is tangible enough to analyze, but the route from product success to token appreciation remains indirect.

My conclusion is conditional, not dismissive. I think RUNE deserves attention as a potentially mispriced infrastructure token. The supply overhang is smaller, the protocol remains useful, and there is a credible path to a rerating if organic activity compounds. But the most persuasive version of that story has not yet been established by the available data.

What stands out to me is not how far RUNE has fallen. It is the distance between what THORChain demonstrably does and what RUNE holders can demonstrably capture. If that gap narrows, the valuation could become compelling. If it does not, the apparent bargain may be an illusion. That is the distinction I would want to resolve before mistaking a battered price for an investment thesis.
