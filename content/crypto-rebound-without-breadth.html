Crypto’s rebound has a participation problem

A brief improvement in market breadth does not erase persistent ETF withdrawals or a defensive technical structure.

November 25, 2026

A market can bounce without recovering. On November 25, the distinction mattered more to me than the direction of Bitcoin’s latest price tick. Participation briefly improved, then deteriorated again, while the longer-term structure remained weak.

Reported global turnover reached $396.52 billion over 24 hours. Yet only eight of 58 assets in the fixed sample were positive. Trading activity is not necessarily buying conviction. A busy market can also be a market in which investors are trying to leave.

The rebound lacked breadth

Global capitalization stood near $2.102 trillion, down 2.02% over 24 hours, with Bitcoin dominance at 55.89%, according to CoinPaprika. The provisional sample excludes identified stablecoins, wrapped assets and staking receipts. It is not a comprehensive top-100 index.

Institutional infrastructure is not institutional buying

Bitcoin and Ethereum spot ETF flows show five consecutive completed sessions of withdrawals through November 24. Bitcoin funds lost a net $2.359 billion over five sessions and $5.427 billion over 20; Ethereum funds lost $417.3 million and $1.502 billion respectively. The unfinished November 25 session is excluded. DefiLlama republishes the latest figures, not independent corroboration.

That does not negate institutional adoption. SBI’s Solana joint venture announcement on November 25 is a constructive longer-term development. But it does not quantify incremental demand for SOL. VanEck’s custody transition filing likewise describes an operational change rather than ETF liquidation.

Leverage does not explain everything

On OKX around 15:03 UTC, Bitcoin and Ethereum perpetual funding estimates were positive (0.00452% and 0.00359%), while Solana’s was negative (−0.00593%). The preceding settled rates were all positive. Perpetual marks were below matched spot prices by 4.65, 5.12 and 7.37 basis points. Open interest was $1.85 billion, $1.10 billion and $203 million on that venue, respectively.

Those observations cannot distinguish new leverage from short covering or spot demand. A historical Bitcoin spot order book at 15:04 UTC showed a tight 0.0171 basis-point spread, but its 20 returned levels per side did not establish complete depth within even ten basis points. A tight spread is not a guarantee of executable liquidity. Aligned native open-interest changes, signed trade flow and liquidation observations would help resolve the competing explanations.

Technical repair remains absent

All twelve sampled core technical series closed below their 20- and 50-period moving averages. Bitcoin and Ethereum daily RSI readings were below 30. Oversold is a description of momentum, not a timing signal. Weekly session conventions and the underlying historical observations warrant caution.

Capital contraction and selective growth

DefiLlama reported stablecoin capitalization of $280.31 billion on November 25. Its prior-day snapshot showed a 1.11% weekly decline and 5.73% monthly decline. Aggregate DeFi TVL was $68.48 billion, including $36.09 billion on Ethereum and $4.53 billion on Solana. Reported daily decentralized spot and perpetual turnover reached $6.89 billion and $25.07 billion respectively. Falling dollar-denominated TVL may reflect falling collateral prices rather than withdrawals; protocol revenue is not automatically token-holder income.

Venice Token’s seven-day gain of 18.18% contrasted with Bitcoin’s −8.01%. Pieverse and JST also gained over the week. Their AI inference, agent payments and lending-related economic theses warrant investigation, but adoption, supply, governance and liquidity questions remain unresolved. Relative strength alone is not an investment ranking.

Macro conditions remain restrictive

The October 28 Federal Reserve decision left the target range at 3.75–4.00%. November 24 Treasury yields were 4.63% at two years and 4.91% at ten years. October CPI rose 0.4% month over month; headline and core PCE prices rose 0.3%, while real consumption was unchanged, according to the BLS and BEA. Nasdaq fell 1.48% on November 24. This is consistent with weak risk appetite, not proof of a causal relationship.

The Thanksgiving closure and shortened November 27 session complicate comparisons of ETF flows. The December 4 jobs report, December 8–9 FOMC meeting and December 10 CPI release are important scheduled tests. See the NYSE calendar and FOMC calendar.

What would change my interpretation?

I would look for sustained recovery above nearby technical boundaries, a majority of the same 58 assets turning positive, and a reversal in completed-session ETF withdrawals. Bitcoin and Solana had already breached their seven-day lower boundaries at the final venue snapshot. Those levels require a reclaim rather than being treated as intact support.

A confirmed close above the upper boundary and subsequent hold would strengthen the bull case. An intact range supports a neutral interpretation; a confirmed exit invalidates it. A close below the lower boundary and failed reclaim weakens the structure. Monthly boundaries are distant historical references, not immediate objectives. No calibrated probabilities are justified.

Until participation, institutional flows and price structure start telling a consistent story, I regard a rebound as evidence that the market can bounce. I do not regard it as evidence that the market has healed.
