QUID, EDGE and KOR: When Product Success Fails to Reach the Token

A useful product can attract customers without creating a valuable token. Three new launches expose the difference.

November 10, 2026 · Market figures are dated snapshots, not live quotes · USD

A cryptocurrency can solve a real problem, build an audience and still disappoint its token holders. That is the uncomfortable possibility at the center of my examination of three 2026 launches: Squid's QUID, edgeX's EDGE and Kor Protocol's KOR. Each has a plausible business purpose. But a product's users, a protocol's revenue and the value of a circulating token are three different things.

I began with a simple three-part screen: Is the utility real? Is there credible evidence of product-market fit? And can token ownership benefit economically without relying exclusively on the next buyer paying more? The deeper I looked, the less useful the conventional headline market capitalization became. Vesting schedules, treasury allocations, buyback accounting and tradable liquidity can change the conclusion.

My working conclusion is deliberately narrower than a buy recommendation: EDGE shows the clearest documented token-demand mechanism; QUID shows meaningful product use but has not established a recurring claim on that activity; and KOR's reported commercial traction has to be weighed against unresolved circulating-supply and concentration questions.

The essential distinction: I am evaluating the economic claim represented by each token—not making an assertion that every useful protocol must appreciate.

Three valuations, three different questions

The November 10 reference snapshots illustrate why a microcap label can conceal important differences. QUID's roughly $7.41 million circulating capitalization corresponds to a fully diluted value near $51.8 million. EDGE's roughly $136.72 million circulating capitalization implies approximately $390.6 million fully diluted. KOR's reported $1.36 million circulating figure depends on a contested, self-reported supply estimate; its approximately $5.96 million fully diluted valuation is a more defensible provisional reference.

QUID: The service is used. Does the token collect the value?

Squid addresses a genuine problem: moving value between different blockchains remains cumbersome for users and developers. Its routing service aims to abstract that complexity. The official statistics dashboard reported $22.38 million in routed volume across 678,280 transactions and 53,572 users in the referenced 30-day window. These are meaningful indications of activity, though first-party aggregated figures are not the same as an independently reconstructed transaction ledger.

Annualizing that routing volume gives roughly $272.3 million. That may sound impressive beside QUID's market capitalization, but it would be a serious error to describe it as annual revenue. Routing volume is customer throughput; the protocol may retain only a small and variable portion after network costs, integrations and other expenses.

The harder question is whether fees create enforceable or mechanically recurring demand for QUID. The published tokenomics describe staking incentives funded from allocated supply and potential buybacks, but indicate buybacks were not active at launch. The research did not establish a verified later activation or an executed-buyback ledger.

An August 2027 supply test

Investor, team and strategic-partner allocations collectively represent about 64.34% of QUID's billion-token supply. The documented first significant insider cliff in August 2027 would release an estimated 160.86 million QUID—approximately 112% of the currently reported circulating token count. At the November 10 indicative price, the release would have a notional value of about $8.33 million.

That is not a forecast of immediate selling. A vesting event changes eligibility to transfer, not necessarily economic intent. But an investment thesis that uses circulating capitalization and ignores this potential liquid-supply expansion is incomplete.

What would change my view: traceable fee revenue, sustained user retention and recurring revenue-funded token purchases large enough to matter against the vesting calendar. Without that, Squid's product could thrive while QUID underperforms.

EDGE: An operating business with a more direct token mechanism

edgeX operates a derivatives trading venue. Its attraction in this comparison is not merely reported trading volume; it is an explicit policy intended to connect perpetual-contract profits with EDGE repurchases and burning. The official tokenomics dashboard reports 5.13% of the original one-billion-token supply repurchased or burned. That implies approximately 51.3 million tokens—but the investigation did not independently match the quantity to finalized burn transactions.

The revenue trend is less reassuring. DefiLlama's reported gross trading fees fell from approximately $76.24 million in the first quarter of 2026 to $35.16 million in the second and $12.29 million in the third. The partial fourth-quarter observation was about $2.76 million, and cannot be compared to complete quarters as though it were a final result. The provider's definitions and accounting periods also are not audited financial statements.

EDGE's second-quarter reported buybacks of approximately $45.09 million exceeded its $21.78 million in retained revenue during that period. This is not necessarily contradictory: purchases could draw on prior accumulated resources or reflect reporting timing. It does, however, rule out casually equating one quarter's buybacks with that quarter's earnings.

There is a second accounting question. The issuer's presentation of 29.87% net circulating supply after reported repurchases differs from an aggregator's 35% circulating estimate. The approximately 51.3 million-token gap is consistent with the reported repurchase amount, but the evidence does not yet show whether the relevant holdings were permanently burned, custodied in a noncirculating wallet or excluded under another supply methodology.

My preferred metric here is not gross buyback spending. It is newly liquid tokens minus permanently retired tokens, measured over time and checked against retained revenue. EDGE has the best documented value-capture design of the three; that is not proof its net liquid supply is actually contracting or that future buyback capacity will recover.

KOR: The smallest valuation may carry the largest evidentiary burden

Kor Protocol's proposed infrastructure for creative-rights verification, licensing and royalty settlement has recognizable real-world customers in principle. The project reports more than one million lifetime sign-ups, over one thousand IP partners and over $2 million in cumulative gross revenue on its official site. These claims warrant attention. They do not yet demonstrate recurring on-chain royalty settlement, revenue collected by the KOR token or cash flows attributable to token ownership.

A second concern is supply quality. The November 10 investigation found CoinMarketCap's roughly 2.287 billion-token circulating estimate marked self-reported, while other sources did not substantiate the same quantity. Separate third-party holder snapshots suggested unusually high concentration among the largest addresses, including investor- and team-sized wallets. Contractually locked wallets may explain much of this concentration; without contract restrictions and address attribution, it is premature to treat those balances as freely tradeable—or safe from eventual distribution.

Reports of a steep launch-period drawdown and highly variable exchange volume increase the need to examine market-making arrangements and actual bid-side liquidity. None of the available research established synchronized exchange depth or reliable $10,000 and $50,000 exit costs. A low FDV does not compensate for the inability to sell at a published price.

What would change my view: independent confirmation of recurring licensing receipts, settlement demand that genuinely requires KOR, audited release schedules, ownership attribution for the largest addresses and usable liquidity across the real trading venues.

What the blockchains have not yet proved

The investigation identified the advertised token contracts: QUID on Base at 0x1a44233FAe8D50F1AeB3a5d58dd426ff4814Cb53, EDGE on Ethereum at 0xb0076de78dc50581770bba1d211ddc0ad4f2a241, and KOR on Base at 0xD3679306360705ba3580BC4130b56460FB4e6926. The earlier assessment found an upgradeable proxy architecture for EDGE, an administrative control surface worth reviewing but not evidence of an exploit.

However, the assembled material does not contain a finished 90-day transfer reconstruction, independently matched buyback/burn transactions, verified treasury address ownership, synchronized order-book sweeps or comprehensive token-holder cash-flow estimates. Consequently, assertions about verified net burns, exchange inflows, wash trading or true free float would exceed the evidence.

The three tests are not equally difficult

Real utility is often easiest to recognize: a product carries out a task someone values. Product-market fit is harder, demanding sustained usage and ideally repeat business without dependence on temporary token incentives. Appreciation potential is hardest because it depends not just on users or revenue but on what rights, constraints and recurring demand attach to the specific token being purchased.

For QUID, activity is substantial enough to keep the investigation open, but I would require demonstrated value capture before drawing an undervaluation conclusion. For EDGE, I see the strongest economic architecture, offset by falling reported trading fees and unresolved burn accounting. For KOR, I see a potentially useful business proposition whose tiny apparent valuation is overwhelmed by supply and liquidity uncertainty.

None is a verified bargain on the evidence assembled. The next legitimate research advance would be a transaction-hash-level reconciliation of token flows, not a more elaborate price target.
